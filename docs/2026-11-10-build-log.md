## 1. The Media Kit becomes a program

*Morning — the menu.*

The media kit is the one public document the institution sells patronage from — attribution,

## 2. The kit, opened as a deck

*Midday — `src/components/patronage/deck/`.*

The kit now opens with six slides above the detail it already printed, so the argument is read
before the rate card is.

- **Six slides, in order:** the hook (*most aerospace media kits are pay-to-play*), the shift
  (legacy publications against this one), the firewall (attribution, never placement), the
  audience, the calendar, and the ask.
- **It walks itself once** — six seconds a slide — and then hands over: reaching the last
  slide, or a hand on the deck, retires the timer for the session. Swipe, dots and Back/Next
  all move it; a reader who has asked their system for reduced motion is never given autoplay.
- **Nothing is a picture of the offer.** Figures come from `patronConfig`; the three priced
  tiers come from live `SponsorSlot` records through `useDeckTiers`, with the published
  calendar as the fallback — so a slide cannot drift from the rate card printed below it. A
  slot already in place reads *Sponsor in place* rather than a price.
- **The ask lands on the desk, not a second window.** The primary key scrolls to the enquiry
  form in the page; the contact line sits under it.
- **Furniture reused, not invented:** the deck's keys are the house `desk-key` family on the
  navy plate; the masthead gold carries section labels, copper carries the structural rules.
- Split into one file per slide plus a frame, so a slide can be rewritten without touching the
  deck's behaviour.

## 3. The contact address on the offer

- `top100aero.space` publishes Mailgun inbound records (`mxa.mailgun.org`, `mxb.mailgun.org`),

  confirmed from outside — only a test send settles it.

  say `partner@top100aero.space`; `src/pages/Solutions.jsx` says `partners@top100aero.space`.
  One address should win across the offer.
